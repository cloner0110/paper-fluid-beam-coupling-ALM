\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Mooring lines, risers, aquaculture netting, textile yarns, and aquatic vegetation all have a cross-section orders of magnitude smaller than the flow domain they occupy, and they all deform under the flow that loads them. Resolving such a body with a boundary-fitted mesh imposes severe local refinement, and the mesh must then be deformed or regenerated at every time step as the structure moves. The cost becomes prohibitive for a canopy of vegetation stems or a net of many strands.

Distributed forcing methods avoid that cost by representing the structure through a lower-dimensional description and introducing its action on the fluid as regularised momentum sources on a fixed Eulerian mesh. The immersed boundary method \citep{peskin_flow_1972,peskin_immersed_2002,mittal_immersed_2005,uhlmann_immersed_2005} established the idea for surfaces, and the actuator line method of \citet{sorensen_numerical_2002} reduced it further for slender members by concentrating the hydrodynamic interaction at actuator points along a curve. Developed for wind-turbine wakes, where it reproduces the essential momentum exchange on meshes far coarser than blade-resolved calculations \citep{troldborg_numerical_2010}, it has since been extended with improved sampling, smoothing, and generalised actuator-curve representations \citep{churchfield_advanced_2017,jha_actuator_2018,navarro_diaz_actuator_2023}. Nothing in the concept restricts it to rotating blades. \citet{bral_development_2025} adapted it to flexible yarns in air-jet weaving and \citet{bral_modeling_2025} extended that formulation into a two-way coupled framework driving a beam model, while \citet{schmitt_coupled_2022} coupled an actuator line to a finite element solver in OpenFOAM for flexible turbine blades and fish-farm nets.

Two operators govern the accuracy of such a coupling. The first is velocity sampling, for which the usual choices are point, integral, and line-average strategies \citep{sorensen_numerical_2002,merabet_parametric_2019,zormpa_effect_2025}. The distinction matters most for drag-dominated members, whose load is aligned with the relative velocity, so that the regularised source acts as a local sink slowing the fluid in the same region where the velocity would be sampled. Because the drag scales with the square of that velocity, an underestimate feeds back on itself. \citet{bral_development_2025} broke this feedback by sampling at a fixed distance upstream of the actuator point, outside the momentum-deficit region, and the same concern underlies the sampling and smearing corrections of \citet{churchfield_modeling_2015}, \citet{martinez_tossas_optimal_2017} and \citet{muscari_effective_2024}. The second operator is force regularisation, since a point force cannot be applied on a finite volume mesh without severe grid dependence and is instead spread with a Gaussian kernel of width $\varepsilon$. A kernel that is too narrow makes the source grid-localised, and one that is too wide weakens the local effect of the line \citep{martinez_tossas_optimal_2017,navarro_diaz_actuator_2023}. Beyond accuracy, the two operators determine which discrete properties the coupling preserves, namely the conservation of exchanged momentum, the resolution of the kernel relative to the local grid, and invariance under parallel domain decomposition \citep{bral_development_2025}.

On the structural side, the geometrically exact beam theory of \citet{reissner_finite_1981}, extended to three dimensions by Simo, treats finite rotations without linearisation and retains shear and torsion alongside bending and axial stretch. Most implementations of it are finite element. Numerical studies of flexible vegetation illustrate what follows from that choice, coupling Eulerian flow solvers to finite element rods \citep{chen_eulerianlagrangian_2019,elrahi_numerical_2023}, immersed-boundary soft bodies \citep{kim_steady_2024}, direct-forcing solvers \citep{pruter_comprehensive_2025}, and articulated bodies \citep{dickinson_modelling_2025}. In every case the structure is described by a different numerical method from the flow, so the two sides rest on different assumptions about how the governing equations are integrated, hold their data in different structures, and are decomposed differently across processors, and each must be verified in its own setting before the coupled result can be relied upon. A cell-centred finite volume discretisation of the same beam theory \citep{bali_cell-centered_2022,bali_finite_2024,bali_beamfoam_2025} removes that separation, because the two subsystems then share one set of conservation statements, one mesh and field infrastructure, one domain decomposition, and one time loop. Two-way actuator-line coupling has been demonstrated for flexible marine structures, but on finite element or external structural solvers \citep{schmitt_coupled_2022,bral_modeling_2025}, so the sampling, regularisation, and parallel exchange have not been established for a geometrically exact finite volume beam.

The hydrodynamic closure adopted here is a quasi-steady Morison-type drag law with no added-mass or history term, and a closure of this kind has an applicability boundary that can be stated in advance. \citet{leclercq_reconfiguration_2018} showed that the character of the wave-induced response of a flexible blade is governed by the combination $CaL/KC$, which is proportional to the squared ratio of the natural period of the fluid-loaded blade to the wave period. Below a threshold of approximately $0.2$ the blade responds quasi-statically in a single spatial mode, and above it the added mass of the surrounding water amplifies the motion and excites higher modes. \citet{jacobsen_waveinduced_2019} confirmed this experimentally for single vegetation mimics and derived drag coefficients by fitting a relative-velocity Morison formulation to the measured base force. Those fitted coefficients carry a documented spread between repeat tests, so a validation that fixes the coefficient in advance should propagate that band through the simulation rather than tune a single value against the response being validated.

This paper formulates and implements a two-way actuator-line coupling between a cell-centred finite volume Simo--Reissner beam and an incompressible finite volume flow solver, and verifies and validates it against three reference problems of increasing difficulty. The contributions are the coupling itself, in which no external structural code is required because the beam shares the mesh, fields, time loop, and domain decomposition of the flow solver; sampling and projection operators that are parallel consistent on a domain-decomposed fluid mesh, with a normalisation that makes the transferred resultant force exact to round-off independently of the mesh, the kernel width, and the kernel truncation; a formulation independent of the number of fluid phases, used unchanged in single-phase channel flow and in a two-phase wave flume; and a verification and validation campaign in which every drag coefficient is fixed in advance, the two operator settings with a physical rather than a purely numerical influence on the response are identified, and the accuracy of the closure together with the range of conditions over which it holds is quantified rather than assumed. Verification and validation are used throughout in the standard sense \citep{roache_quantification_1997,oberkampf_roy_2010}, the first asking whether the coupled equations are solved correctly and established against independent numerical solutions, the second asking whether the model reproduces physical reality and established against experiment.

Section~\ref{sec:mathmodel} presents the governing equations and the hydrodynamic closure, and Section~\ref{sec:numericalmodel} the finite volume beam discretisation and the coupling operators. Sections~\ref{sec:cantilever} to~\ref{sec:leaflet} verify the structural solver and then the coupling, against a flexible beam in a steady confined channel and a leaflet in a sinusoidal channel flow. Section~\ref{sec:flexveg} validates the coupling against the wave-flume experiment of \citet{jacobsen_waveinduced_2019} on a flexible vegetation stem, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} draws the conclusions.
